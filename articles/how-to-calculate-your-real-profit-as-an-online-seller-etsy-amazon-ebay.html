How to Calculate Your Real Profit as an Online Seller (Etsy, Amazon, eBay)

You sold a product for $30. You feel good. But how much did you actually keep? If you're like most online sellers, the honest answer is "I'm not totally sure." Between platform fees, payment processing, shipping, packaging, ad spend, and the cost of the product itself, the number that lands in your bank account is a lot smaller than the sale price — and a surprising number of sellers are quietly losing money on items they think are winners.

This guide walks you through how to calculate your real profit per sale and per month, with actual numbers, so you can price correctly and stop guessing.

Why "Sale Price Minus Product Cost" Is a Trap

The math most sellers do in their head looks like this:

I bought it for $10, I sold it for $30, so I made $20.

That's not profit. That's gross margin before fees — and the fees are where the money disappears. Every marketplace takes a cut, the payment processor takes a cut, shipping costs real money, and your supplies aren't free. Your real profit is what's left after all of it.

Here's the full chain of costs you need to subtract from every sale:

  • Cost of goods sold (COGS): what you paid for the product or the materials to make it.
  • Marketplace fees: listing fees, transaction/referral fees, and category fees.
  • Payment processing fees: usually a percentage plus a flat per-transaction amount.
  • Shipping cost: what you pay the carrier, even if the buyer "paid shipping."
  • Packaging and supplies: boxes, mailers, tape, labels, tissue, thank-you cards.
  • Advertising: Etsy Ads, Amazon PPC, eBay Promoted Listings.
  • Refunds, returns, and fees you don't get back.

The Platform Fees You're Actually Paying

Fees change, so always confirm current rates in your seller dashboard. But here are the typical structures so you know what to look for.

Etsy

  • Listing fee: about $0.20 per item listed.
  • Transaction fee: around 6.5% of the item price (and often the shipping you charge).
  • Payment processing: roughly 3% + $0.25 per order in the US (varies by country).
  • Offsite Ads fee: 12–15% on sales that come through Etsy's offsite ads, charged only when one converts.

Amazon

  • Referral fee: typically 8–15% depending on category (15% is common).
  • FBA fees (if you use Fulfillment by Amazon): pick-and-pack plus storage, based on size and weight.
  • Monthly Professional plan: about $39.99/month if you sell at volume.

eBay

  • Final value fee: roughly 13.25% on most categories, applied to the total amount including shipping, plus about $0.30 per order.
  • Insertion fees beyond your free listing allotment.
  • Promoted Listings: an ad rate you set, charged on sales driven by promotion.

The key insight: most platforms charge their percentage on the total the buyer pays — item plus shipping plus tax in some cases — not just the item price. That quietly raises your effective fee rate.

A Real Example: One Etsy Sale, Start to Finish

Let's run a handmade candle sold on Etsy for $30, with $6 shipping charged to the buyer.

Line item Amount
Item price $30.00
Shipping charged to buyer $6.00
Total buyer pays $36.00
Listing fee -$0.20
Transaction fee (6.5% of $36) -$2.34
Payment processing (3% of $36 + $0.25) -$1.33
Cost of materials (wax, wick, jar, scent) -$7.00
Actual shipping label you bought -$8.50
Packaging (box, filler, label) -$1.25
Total costs -$20.87
Real profit $15.13

So the $30 candle that "cost $7 to make" actually nets about $15 — and that's before you spend a dime on ads. If you ran Offsite Ads and this sale converted through one, subtract another ~$5.40 (15% of $36), and you're down to under $10.

Notice the trap: you charged $6 shipping but the label cost $8.50. You lost $2.50 on shipping alone. Many sellers never catch this because they look at the sale total and feel like they came out ahead.

How to Calculate Profit Margin (Not Just Profit Dollars)

Dollars per sale matter, but margin tells you whether the business is healthy. Use this formula:

Profit Margin = (Real Profit ÷ Total Revenue) × 100

In our example: $15.13 ÷ $36.00 = 42% margin. That's solid for a handmade product. Once ads enter the picture and margin drops below ~20%, you're working hard for very little, and one return can wipe out several sales.

A quick rule of thumb for physical-product sellers:

  • Under 15% margin: fragile. Returns and fee changes can push you negative.
  • 20–40% margin: healthy and sustainable.
  • 40%+ margin: strong; you have room to advertise and discount.

Don't Forget the Costs That Hide Outside a Single Sale

Per-sale math is only half the picture. Each month, also subtract:

  • Subscription/plan fees (Amazon Professional, Etsy Plus, apps and tools).
  • Advertising spend that didn't directly tie to a sale.
  • Sample products, photography, and supplies you bought but didn't sell.
  • Your time. Pay yourself an hourly wage on paper, even if you don't take it out. If a product nets $15 but takes you 90 minutes to make and ship, that's $10/hour before taxes.

Then there's tax. Set aside roughly 25–30% of your net profit for income and self-employment tax so it doesn't ambush you in April. That candle's $15.13 is really closer to $10–11 after you reserve for taxes.

A Simple System You Can Run Every Month

  1. List every product with its sale price and true COGS.
  2. Record the actual fees from your payout reports — don't estimate. Etsy, Amazon, and eBay all let you download transaction CSVs.
  3. Subtract real shipping and packaging, including the gap between what you charged and what the label cost.
  4. Calculate profit dollars and margin per product.
  5. Flag anything under your margin floor (say, 20%) and either raise the price, cut the cost, or stop selling it.
  6. Total it monthly, subtract overhead and ad spend, then reserve for tax.

Do this once and you'll almost certainly find a "best seller" that's barely profitable and a quiet item that's your real moneymaker. That's the whole point: pricing and product decisions get easy once you can see the true number.
